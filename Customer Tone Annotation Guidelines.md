# Customer Tone Annotation Guidelines

@Venkat @Vishnu @Rosh

## Purpose and ground rules

You will label how the **customer** sounds across a whole support conversation, using one of five tone labels. Each item is a conversation between a customer and an Amazon support agent on Twitter. The label describes the customer's tone, not the agent's tone and not how good the support was.

1. **Read the whole conversation first.** Agent replies are context: they explain why a customer reacts the way they do and they expose sarcasm (a "Thanks" after an unhelpful reply is not appreciation).
2. **Judge the customer's words only.** Ignore how the agent sounds and whether the problem was solved.
3. **Pick exactly one label.** If more than one seems to fit, use the order of precedence in Part B.
4. **Label the customer's overall tone across the whole conversation.** Every customer message counts; the last message does not take priority. If different tones appear at different points, use the order of precedence in Part B: the tone higher in the list wins, wherever it appears.
5. **Label what the text shows, not what you imagine the customer feels.** Look at word choice, punctuation, capital letters, emojis, hashtags, questions and demands. Typos and slang are not tone signals by themselves.
6. **Examples are real.** All examples in this guide come from the Amazon support conversations. First names are replaced with \[name\] and links appear as \[link\].

## Part A: Detailed guidelines

The five labels run from calm and positive to angry and demanding, with help-seeking as a side branch for customers who simply need an answer. Decide by what the customer's words are doing: thanking, informing, asking, complaining or demanding. Each label below explains what it means, which signals to look for, how to tell it from its neighbors, and two real conversations.

### 1. Appreciative or positive

The customer expresses gratitude, satisfaction, happiness or praise. After reading the messages, you should feel the customer is pleased, whether with the help received, the product or the service in general. Clear signals are thanks that follow a helpful reply, words such as "awesome", "superb", "love" or "so happy", and positive emojis or exclamations. The praise does not have to be about customer service: a customer enjoying a movie on Prime Video is positive too.

Watch for two traps. First, a polite "thanks" on its own is not enough: "Thanks, I'll try that" is a courtesy, so it is Neutral. Second, sarcastic thanks ("Thanks Amazon... disgusting service") is a complaint, not appreciation, so always read the surrounding messages. If a customer is positive but also asks for something, use the order of precedence in Part B across all of their messages.

**Example 1 (conversation 1024230).** The customer is positive from start to finish.

> **Customer:** if i wud nt hv been a prime member #arjunreddy would have made me 1. #bestmovieonamazonprime wtchd in loop 3 times yday
>
> **Agent:** Hope you liked the movie. #HappyStreaming.
>
> **Customer:** Awesome thanks #amazonprimevideo
>
> **Agent:** You're welcome! :)

*Why Appreciative:* the customer asks for nothing. They volunteer enthusiasm (a hashtag praising the movie, watching it three times) and close with "Awesome thanks". Everything points to satisfaction.

**Example 2 (conversation 2473401), a contrast case.** The customer starts by asking for help and ends delighted. This one is *not* labeled Appreciative.

> **Customer:** Can't find a place to pre-order the Dunkirk Filmbook special edition blu-ray to be delivered to Ireland. Can somebody help me 😔 #dunkirk
>
> **Agent:** Hello \[name\], we can ship this item to Ireland and is available to pre-order here: \[link\]. Thanks.
>
> **Customer:** I'm so happy 😢 thank you thank you thank you
>
> **Agent:** Glad we could help 😊

*Why Help-seeking, not Appreciative:* the last message carries strong happiness ("I'm so happy", "thank you" three times), but the first message is a calm request for help ("Can somebody help me"). Every customer message counts, and Help-seeking comes before Appreciative in the order of precedence, so the primary label is Help-seeking. Choose Appreciative as the optional secondary label.

### 2. Neutral or informational

The customer states facts, answers the agent's questions or supplies details without visible emotion, without asking anything and without demanding anything. The text reads like a report: what was ordered, which website, what the tracking says, a date, a one-word confirmation such as "I did".

Describing a problem is not automatically a complaint. A customer can say a parcel is late in a flat, factual way, and that is still Neutral. Move to Frustrated only when the wording shows displeasure: negative adjectives, sarcasm, capital letters, exclamation marks, repeated "still", or evaluative remarks about the company. Move to Help-seeking when the customer asks a question or asks for help.

**Example 1 (conversation 166430).**

> **Customer:** I made an order on Wednesday and selected One day shipping and the package did not arrive yesterday, and has yet to arrive today.
>
> **Agent:** I'm sorry you haven't received your order as anticipated; we'd like to help. Did you place this order on \[link\] (.co.uk, .ca)?
>
> **Customer:** I ordered it from the Canadian website (.ca).
>
> **Agent:** Thanks for clarifying! We'd like to look into this with you. Please reach out to us via phone or chat here: \[link\]

*Why Neutral:* both customer messages are plain statements of fact. There are no emotion words, no question marks and no demands, even though a delivery is late.

**Example 2 (conversation 2922471).** This one sits close to the line.

> **Customer:** hi I was due a refund from 2 purchases as didn't receive items , refund still hasn't gone in and it's been more than 10 days
>
> **Agent:** Sorry this hasn't arrived as expected. Did you receive an e-mail confirming these refunds?
>
> **Customer:** I did
>
> **Agent:** That's weird, \[name\]! We'd like to look into this with you. Please contact us via phone or chat here: \[link\]

*Why Neutral:* the customer reports a missing refund and the number of days, then answers the agent with "I did". There are no evaluative words, no emphasis and no demand. Had the customer written "still no refund after 10 days, ridiculous", it would be Frustrated.

### 3. Help-seeking or asking a question

The customer's main purpose is to get an answer, an instruction or assistance, and the tone is calm or politely neutral. Typical signals are question marks, "how do I", "can you", "what's your policy", "any suggestions", "please help" and "can somebody help". The customer treats the agent as a source of information and is not blaming anyone. A customer may describe a problem first, as long as the message is aimed at getting help.

Watch the edge with Frustrated. If a question is really a complaint ("Why do I pay for Prime when nothing arrives in 2 days anymore?"), it is rhetorical and the label is Frustrated. If the customer demands action by a deadline or asks for a manager, it is Escalatory. A question can mention words such as "problem" or even "scam" and still be Help-seeking when the customer is honestly asking what to do.

**Example 1 (conversation 379709).**

> **Customer:** Hiya - all radio stations cut out regularly using app on Alexa - any suggestions? (works fine on other devices)
>
> **Agent:** Hi \[name\], have you cleared the Data on the App for this particular device ?
>
> **Customer:** No, how do I do it? (it's Alexa device with app on iPad)
>
> **Agent:** With an iPad, you can uninstall and reinstall the app. You may need to login again when reinstalling.

*Why Help-seeking:* the customer describes a technical problem and asks for suggestions, then asks a genuine follow-up question. The tone is friendly ("Hiya") and cooperative, with no blame.

**Example 2 (conversation 873728).**

> **Customer:** what's your return policy on wireless routers?
>
> **Agent:** Thanks for reaching out! You can find our return policy here: \[link\]
>
> **Customer:** Which one does a router fall under?
>
> **Agent:** A router would fall under our standard 30 day return policy. We'll be here if you have any other questions!

*Why Help-seeking:* both customer messages are plain questions. There is no problem to complain about and no emotion; the customer only needs information.

### 4. Frustrated or complaining

The customer is clearly unhappy, annoyed, disappointed or critical, but is not pressing for immediate action and is not threatening anything. The customer is venting about the company, a product or a policy. Typical signals are negative judgments ("lame", "horrible", "a joke"), mild profanity, sarcasm, rhetorical questions, capital letters for emphasis, repeated punctuation ("!!", "?!") and remarks that a problem keeps happening ("still waiting", "again").

To separate it from its neighbors, ask three things. Is there evaluative or emotional wording? If not, it is Neutral. Is the question rhetorical, or is the real point the complaint? If so, it is Frustrated and not Help-seeking. Does the customer demand something, threaten something or accuse the company? If so, it is Escalatory.

**Example 1 (conversation 785097).**

> **Customer:** you show lockers in Toronto but the app only lets you select pick up points
>
> **Agent:** Please reach out here so we can help you out \[name\] \[link\]
>
> **Customer:** so you put 5 lockers on the far side of the GTA but you cant put any in Toronto proper? lame
>
> **Agent:** Please use this link to locate your nearest Locker \[link\]

*Why Frustrated:* the customer criticizes a service gap with a sarcastic, rhetorical question and the word "lame". Nothing is demanded and nothing is threatened.

**Example 2 (conversation 1190828).**

> **Customer:** Hey! ! ! How the hell do I get Twitch Prime WITHOUT signing up for Amazon Prime?!
>
> **Agent:** Hi, Twitch Prime is one of the many benefits of Amazon Prime! Here's more info about signing up: \[link\].
>
> **Customer:** I know I can get Twitch Prime with Amazon Prime. I don't WANT Amazon Prime! I want a cheaper TWITCH PRIME ONLY OPTION
>
> **Agent:** Please reach out to us directly here: \[link\] and we can look into this further.

*Why Frustrated:* it opens with a question, but "How the hell", repeated exclamation marks and capitalized words show irritation. The customer is unhappy with a pricing policy and is not demanding an urgent fix or threatening anything.

### 5. Escalatory or requesting immediate resolution

The customer has moved beyond venting and is pressing for action or raising the stakes. Use this label when at least one of these is present:

1. A demand for immediate action or for a specific person, such as "have a manager call me", "I need a human", "ASAP" or "now".
2. A threat of consequences: a lawsuit, cancelling Prime or an order, reporting the company, going public or switching to a competitor.
3. An accusation aimed at the company: lying, fraud, theft or cheating.
4. Anger that intensifies across the conversation, with repeated strong condemnation, all capitals or strings of "!!!".

Escalation can sit in one message or build over several. The key difference from Frustrated is that frustrated customers complain, while escalating customers push or threaten. Strong language alone ("pathetic") stays Frustrated unless one of the four signs above is also present. A calm but firm demand ("I need this resolved today or I will dispute the charge") is Escalatory despite its polite wording.

**Example 1 (conversation 1273449).**

> **Customer:** Your email support for amazon Flex is horrible. I need help to get the money I worked for!!!! PLEASE HELP ME !
>
> **Agent:** Please submit any payment questions via the Flex mobile app. Here's some FAQs that might help: \[link\]
>
> **Customer:** Again with the generic responses. I submitted via the app but i am only getting prewritten respones back. I need a human to help me !!!
>
> **Agent:** We'd like to look into this for you. Please send us your details here: \[link\]
>
> **Customer:** Can you have a manger Call me ? This is ridiculous. You guys owe me money that I worked for. Do I have to file a lawsuit?

*Why Escalatory:* the customer asks for a manager to call, says they need a human after generic replies, and ends by asking whether they must file a lawsuit. That is a demand (signal 1) plus a threat (signal 2).

**Example 2 (conversation 1532184).** This one is closer to the line.

> **Customer:** Thanks Amazon... "guaranteed next day" to be told it'll arrive on the 8th. Disgusting treatment by customer service team
>
> **Agent:** Sorry to hear that! What options or insight was provided when you contacted us?
>
> **Customer:** To wait 5 days for delivery!!! No explanation just useless apologies
>
> **Agent:** I completely understand your frustration! Please let us know if you don't get your order by November 8th.
>
> **Customer:** Unacceptable. Absolutely unacceptable. I don't pay for Amazon prime to get my items a week later

*Why Escalatory:* the opening "Thanks Amazon..." is sarcasm, not appreciation. The anger then grows with each message: "Disgusting", then "!!!", then "Unacceptable. Absolutely unacceptable.", ending with the customer saying they are not getting what they pay for. No manager or lawsuit is mentioned, so this sits at the milder end of Escalatory and is labeled by signal 4, anger that intensifies across the conversation.

## Part B: Quick reference

### The five labels

- **Appreciative or positive**
  - Meaning: the customer is pleased, grateful or praising.
  - Cues: "thank you very much", "awesome", "love it", "so happy", praise, positive emojis.
  - Not this label if: the thanks is only a courtesy ("Thanks, will try") → Neutral; the thanks is sarcastic → Frustrated or Escalatory.
- **Neutral or informational**
  - Meaning: the customer states facts or answers questions with no visible emotion, question or demand.
  - Cues: order details, dates, tracking status, "I did", "it's the .ca site".
  - Not this label if: there is evaluative wording or emphasis → Frustrated; there is a question or request for help → Help-seeking.
- **Help-seeking or asking a question**
  - Meaning: the customer wants an answer, instructions or assistance, in a calm tone.
  - Cues: question marks, "how do I", "can you", "any suggestions", "please help".
  - Not this label if: the question is rhetorical or angry → Frustrated; it demands action or a manager → Escalatory.
- **Frustrated or complaining**
  - Meaning: the customer is unhappy or critical but is not pressing or threatening.
  - Cues: "lame", "horrible", "a joke", sarcasm, rhetorical questions, capitals for emphasis, "?!", "still waiting".
  - Not this label if: there is no emotional wording → Neutral; there is a demand, threat or accusation → Escalatory.
- **Escalatory or requesting immediate resolution**
  - Meaning: the customer presses for action or raises the stakes.
  - Cues: "manager", "I need a human", "ASAP", "lawsuit", "cancel my Prime", "fraud", "liar", repeated "Unacceptable", "!!!".
  - Not this label if: the customer only uses strong words with no demand, threat, accusation or build-up → Frustrated.

### Order of precedence

When the customer's messages show more than one tone anywhere in the conversation, take the first label in this list that applies:

1. **Escalatory** if there is a demand, a threat, an accusation or anger that has clearly intensified.
2. **Frustrated** if there is negative emotion or a complaint and no demand or threat.
3. **Help-seeking** if the customer asks a question or requests help and shows no negative emotion.
4. **Appreciative** if the thanks or praise is genuine and not sarcastic.
5. **Neutral** if none of the above applies.

### Edge cases

- **Sarcasm:** "Thanks a lot..." after a bad reply is negative. Label it Frustrated, or Escalatory if a demand, threat, accusation or build-up is also present.
- **Mixed tone:** every customer message counts, so use the order of precedence. A customer who asks for a manager early and later says thanks is Escalatory. You can pick the other tone as the optional secondary label.
- **Angry question:** a question with an angry edge is Frustrated or Escalatory, not Help-seeking.
- **Problem described flatly:** a late order or missing refund stated without emotion is Neutral.
- **Agent behavior:** ignore it, except to understand the customer's reaction.
- **Hashtags and emojis:** count them as tone signals (a praising hashtag is positive; "#fraud" aimed at Amazon is an accusation).
- **Unreadable or unclear text** (only a link or image, heavy slang, mixed languages): label what you can read with confidence. If you cannot judge the tone, choose Neutral and flag the item if the tool allows it.
